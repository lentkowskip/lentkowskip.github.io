About this framework orientation for new readers · click to expand

This dashboard tracks physical oil market indicators to test macro narratives against actual barrel flows. The thesis: financial press lags physical signals by days to weeks, so during a Mideast disruption you can read recession-transmission risk from tanker rates, war-risk insurance premiums, regional spreads, and refinery utilization before futures or equity desks catch up.

Two things that will confuse you in three months

Substitution-polarity flip. Indicators tagged “Substitution” (TD15 VLCC WAF-China, ESPO premium, US crude exports) are reverse-polarity. Higher = green = engaged channel = absorbing the shock. They look like crisis readings against the default Normal/Elevated/Crisis ladder, but a high reading means substitution is working. Read them through the regime matrix, not the standalone pill.

Dubai-Brent two-mode convention. The spread sign was flipped on Apr 17, 2026. Negative spread = Atlantic-basin tightness (2022 Russia-Ukraine signature). Positive spread = Mideast trapped-barrel scarcity (current Hormuz signature). Two-sided thresholds capture both modes — don’t pattern-match a positive print to the 2022 setup.

For readers new to this what physical vs paper means

What this showsOil has two prices that should move together: the physical price — what real barrels change hands for in tanker deals, regional spreads, and war-risk insurance — and the paper price — what futures contracts trade at on financial screens. This chart tracks whether they're aligned or not.

How to read itThe thickest line — labeled divergence (phys - paper) in the legend — is physical composite stress minus paper composite stress. Above the dashed zero baseline = physical is more stressed than paper (futures haven't caught up to what's happening with actual barrels). Below zero = paper is running ahead of physical (futures are pricing in a shock that hasn't physically arrived). The closer to zero, the more the two markets agree. Dots on the divergence line turn red when the gap is wide (>0.5) and amber when it's elevated (>0.15).

Why it matters for the frameworkDuring a Hormuz crisis, physical stress shows up first — ship insurance, tanker rates, regional crude spreads — and futures follow days-to-weeks later. A persistent positive divergence means futures are underpricing the disruption and paper markets are likely to reprice up. A persistent negative divergence means futures are overshooting — paper correction down is the risk. The divergence itself is the signal, not the absolute levels.

For readers new to this what response capacity means

What this showsWhen a major oil chokepoint like the Strait of Hormuz closes, the question that decides whether the shock transmits to the broader economy isn't just how big is the disruption — it's are the substitute supply channels picking up the slack. This panel tracks three substitution channels: US crude exports (can the US ship more), the West Africa to China tanker rate (is Atlantic crude being rerouted to Asia), and the ESPO premium (is Russian Pacific crude being bid up by Asian buyers).

How to read itThe score is INVERTED versus the divergence panel: higher engagement = better. 0 means substitution channels are idle (no replacement flow); 1 means partial engagement (some channels active, others lagging); 2+ means fully engaged (all channels absorbing the disruption). Each channel scores between 0 and ~2.5 individually, then averaged. Green dots = full engagement; amber = partial; red = idle. Watch the trajectory more than the level — engagement rising = substitution flow building; engagement flat or falling = response capacity exhausted or never engaged.

Why it matters for the frameworkThe divergence panel measures whether physical disruption is leading paper futures. This panel measures whether the disruption has working escape valves. They're complementary: high divergence + low engagement = futures will reprice UP (disruption priced, no relief flowing); high divergence + high engagement = divergence will narrow as substitution catches up (paper repricing follows physical stabilization). For the recession-probability question, low engagement raises the real conditional probability above what the mechanical model prints, because the oil shock has fewer paths to dissipate.

Historical anchors

  • 1973-74 OPEC embargo — Arab states cut ~15% of US supply on political grounds. No Atlantic-to-Asia rerouting infrastructure existed; allies were embargoed too; the SPR didn’t exist yet. Brent Crisis × Sub Idle → stagflation path Brent quadrupled, S&P -25% by week 12, recession Nov 73 – Mar 75.
  • 1979-80 Iranian Revolution — Iranian production collapsed from 5.5M to 0.5M bpd. Saudi swing capacity didn’t replace within timeframe; tanker reroute trade not yet built. Brent Crisis × Sub Idle → stagflation path (canonical). No equity drawdown during the shock itself; Volcker-induced 1980 recession + 1981-82 double-dip followed.
  • 1990-91 Iraq invasion of Kuwait — ~4M bpd Iraqi+Kuwaiti volumes off market overnight. Saudi swing capacity activated within weeks; coordinated IEA strategic-reserve release. Brent Crisis × Sub Engaged → absorption path (canonical). Brent $17→$35-40 then reversed sharply on Desert Storm. Recession was short and shallow, driven by pre-existing bank weakness more than oil.
  • 2014-16 oil collapse — Inverted shock: US shale flooded supply, OPEC declined to cut, Brent fell 75% over 18 months. Out of matrix frame Useful counterfactual: oil moves alone don’t cause US recession when consumer balance sheets, banks, and labor markets are healthy.
  • 2022 Russia-Ukraine — Russian crude self-sanctioned, ~3M bpd flow disruption. Russian barrels rerouted to India and China; Atlantic basin (US Gulf, WAF, Norwegian) flowed to Europe; coordinated SPR releases. Brent Elevated × Sub Engaged → absorbed shock (canonical). Brent held $90-120 for months; equity initial -10% recovered; Fed-tightening was the actual transmission ~7 months later.
For readers new to this how the matrix conditions the transmission read

What this showsThe macro transmission of an oil shock is conditional, not monotonic. A high Brent print can resolve into very different macro paths depending on whether the substitution channels (Atlantic-to-Asia tanker reroute, US export ceiling, Russian Pacific crude bid up) are absorbing the supply gap. This 3x3 matrix is the joint state of Brent regime and substitution regime, mapped to the closest historical analogue.

How to read itThe highlighted cell is the current joint state. Read the transmission and recession panels below as conditional on this cell. Brent Crisis x Sub Engaged = 1990-91 absorption (price spike priced and rerouted; recession risk lower). Brent Crisis x Sub Idle = 1979 stagflation path (price priced, channels blocked, transmission compounds). The cells in between are intermediate. The substitution composite is the lever that moves you between these worlds.

Why it matters for the frameworkWithout this matrix the dashboard reads as if higher TD15 / ESPO / WAF-China readings are stress signals (because the column header defaults to Baseline / Elevated / Crisis). They are not. They are activation signals for the shock-absorption channel, and they only have a stress reading when paired with Brent regime. The matrix makes that conditionality explicit.

For readers new to this what the yen carry trade is

What this showsThe yen carry trade is when investors borrow Japanese yen (cheap) and use the proceeds to buy higher-yielding assets elsewhere — US Treasuries, stocks, crypto. Decades of Japanese near-zero rates made this profitable. When that setup breaks — because Japan hikes rates, or the yen suddenly strengthens — those investors must sell foreign assets fast to repay the yen loans. That's an unwind, and it hits everything dollar-denominated at once. August 2024 was a small one.

How to read itThe score has two parts. Pressure measures the setup: is USD/JPY near MoF intervention levels? Is the US-JP rate differential compressed? Velocity measures the trigger: is the yen moving quickly in the strengthening direction? Multiplying them gives the unwind risk — a loaded spring doesn't matter without a push, and velocity without a loaded setup usually mean-reverts. Above 0.65 = crisis, 0.25-0.65 = elevated, below 0.25 = calm.

Why it matters for the frameworkJapan imports ~90% of its oil through Hormuz. A closure spikes Japanese energy costs, forces BoJ to respond, and can trigger the unwind. This score is an independent cross-check on the oil signals: if oil is flashing crisis but carry stays calm, transmission to global financial markets is delayed and there's more time to position. If carry fires alongside oil, the cascade has already started.

Financial transmission probability · credit + equity joint stress

Mechanical financial-transmission probability model. Forward-looking signal for credit + equity joint-stress events: probability HY OAS breaks 400bp from 285bp current AND/OR S&P drawdown >5% within each window. Probability = (base-rate + oil-shock + carry-unwind) * duration multiplier - substitution-engagement discount. The substitution discount is what makes this forward-looking: high engagement = oil shock has working escape valves = lower transmission probability. The duration multiplier compounds with weeks since physical-disruption onset (Feb 28 2026 US-Israel air war + Hormuz closure) - reflects that sustained closure depletes inventory buffers, breaks charter contracts, and stacks refinery outages. Calibration anchors based on 1973-74, 1990-91, 2008, and 2022 oil-shock analogues. Not backtested. Windows are SHORTER than recession windows because financial transmission is faster than real-economy transmission.

What this showsThis panel estimates how likely the oil shock transmits to broader credit and equity markets within short horizons (4w / 12w / 26w). 'Transmission' here means a joint-stress event: HY OAS breaks 400bp from current 285bp, OR S&P drawdown >5%, OR both. The recession panel below covers slower real-economy effects (3-18 months); this panel covers faster financial-market effects (weeks to months). Together they answer two distinct questions: when do markets crack, and when does the economy crack.

How to read itEach window probability = base rate (weighted leading-indicator stress) + oil shock amplifier + carry unwind amplifier - substitution-engagement discount. The substitution discount is the new piece - it's what makes this forward-looking rather than coincident. When substitution channels are engaged (US exports flowing, freight rates spiking, ESPO compressed), the oil shock has working escape valves and transmission probability falls. When channels are idle, transmission probability rises. Calibration anchors based on 1973-74, 1990-91, 2008, 2022 oil-shock analogues. Not backtested; treat as risk-asymmetry read, not a precise market-timing signal.

Why it matters for the frameworkThis is the panel for the 'when do my equity holdings start to feel this' question. The physical-paper divergence panel says how much paper futures are mispricing the disruption. The substitution panel says whether the shock has somewhere to dissipate. This panel synthesizes both into a probability of credit/equity joint stress over the next weeks-to-months horizon. Best read alongside the recession panel: short-window financial transmission usually precedes longer-window real-economy transmission, so high probabilities here that aren't reflected in the recession panel signal a potential discrepancy worth investigating.

Historical pattern reference

  • 1973-74 OPEC embargo: Recession lag ~14 months. NBER dated Nov 73 - Mar 75; real economic damage built gradually after embargo. Pattern: oil shock → inflation pass-through → Volcker tightening → recession.
  • 1979-80 Iranian Revolution: Recession lag ~24 months. Recession arrived early 1980, then double-dip 1981-82 - both Volcker-induced. Pattern: oil shock alone did not cause recession; Fed reaction did.
  • 1990-91 Iraq invasion of Kuwait: Recession lag ~4 months. Recession arrived Dec 1990, only ~4 months after Aug invasion. Pattern: banks were weak going in; oil shock was the catalyst, not the cause.
  • 2008 GFC: Recession preceded the oil peak. NBER dates recession from Dec 2007; Brent peaked July 2008. Pattern: financial-system-led recession with oil amplifying - not a clean oil-to-recession lag.
  • 2014-16 oil collapse: No recession despite 18 months of -75% Brent. Pattern: oil shock without macro-system stress (banks, consumers, leverage) does not cause US recession.
  • 2022-23 Russia-Ukraine: No recession through 2+ years post-shock. Despite oil shock plus Fed tightening, US avoided recession. Pattern: structural buffers (consumer balance sheets, residual fiscal stimulus) prevented technical recession.

Framework structure

Dashboard tracks 42 indicators across Upstream (Mideast disruption + US response), Downstream (gas, naphtha crack, fertilizer, dry-bulk freight), and Financial (carry unwind, credit, leading-economic indicators). Composite scores synthesize these into four headline reads: physical-paper divergence, substitution-response engagement, carry unwind, and financial transmission probability. The recession-probability windows synthesize across all three categories.

Composite-score methodology

Physical-paper divergence: Average physical-stress score minus average paper-stress score across Mideast-disruption indicators. Spread-tagged indicators (DFL, Dubai-Brent, ESPO) excluded to avoid double-counting. Positive = physical leads paper.

Substitution-response engagement: Average engagement score across US crude exports, TD15 freight, and ESPO premium. Inverted polarity vs other composites: higher = more engaged = lower macro risk transmission.

Carry unwind: Pressure (USD/JPY level + US-JP 10Y diff stress) multiplied by (0.3 + 0.7 * Velocity) where Velocity = USD/JPY 30d change stress. Loaded-spring formulation.

Financial transmission probability: Per-window probability = base rate (weighted leading-indicator stress) + oil-shock amplifier + carry-unwind amplifier - substitution-engagement discount. Windows 4w / 12w / 26w. Calibration anchors based on 1973-74, 1990-91, 2008, 2022 oil-shock analogues. Not backtested.

Recession probability: Same architecture as transmission but with 3mo / 3-9mo / 9-18mo windows tied to leading-indicator base rates (LEI, SLOOS, CC delinq, BDC P/B). Compared against three third-party models (NY Fed yield curve, Conference Board LEI rule, Sahm Rule).
